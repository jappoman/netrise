# 01. Vision, setting and product boundaries

## One sentence
A retrofuturist cyberpunk, single-player incremental strategy game about two asymmetric forces: **Runners steal access and secrets; Corporations build, monetize and defend them**.

## Design fantasy
**Runner:** an anonymous operator in a rain-drenched apartment, growing from a salvaged terminal to an underground intelligence network. Resources are scarce, assets are concealed and every high-value intrusion risks exposure.

**Corporation:** a junior infrastructure firm growing into a data empire. Profit, uptime, security, reputation and secrecy are in conflict. Hidden assets and defenses frustrate runners. Corporate choices must have proactive objectives beyond merely waiting for attacks.

## Visual identity
1980s imagined-future, analog CRT noir. Rain, smog, fluorescent boardrooms, terminal phosphor, tape archives, industrial hum, serialized reports, pseudonymous street contacts. Runner palette: soft green, cyan, rust red. Corp palette: amber, warm white, crimson alert. Avoid tiny unreadable text, forced flicker or unskippable typing effects. No literal copyrighted Blade Runner or Android: Netrunner lore.

## Player and platform
- Desktop keyboard-first, Linux/Windows/macOS terminals where supported.
- Steam edition later via dedicated terminal host/launcher that runs the identical TUI, with no dependency on user's installed shell.
- Offline single-player. Sessions of 5–20 minutes with idle progress between sessions. Active fights generally 15–90 seconds in the first hour.
- No scripting knowledge required. Commands and hotkeys are optional convenience, not a prerequisite.

## Six pillars
1. **True asymmetry:** Corp manufactures value and constructs secrets; Runner learns where value lies and raids it.
2. **Preparation is gameplay:** spend effort gathering intel, building forces, choosing posture.
3. **Simulation is spectacle:** readable, continuous numerical combat with visible causality.
4. **Exponential economy with meaningful constraints:** no mindless single-button spam.
5. **Automation as progression:** tedious early tasks become autonomous; advanced decisions remain interesting.
6. **Terminal authenticity:** use the capabilities and limitations of a real TUI intentionally.

## Guardrails
- Not a puzzle hacking simulator, real intrusion toolkit, CCG clone, social MMO, RTS with micro-management or generic colored-territory map.
- No theft or punishment during closed/offline time.
- Corporation targets should feel like institutions with capital, security, business sectors and confidential projects; not anonymous RPG monsters.
- Runner attacks seek **data, access, money or exposure**; nodes represent assets/connections rather than territorial tiles.
- A single world simulation can feed both campaigns but each run starts as one faction. Cross-save PvP is out of scope.
- Never sell power; premium Steam title is the tentative business model, subject to validation.

## Why these references
Cookie Clicker: generator milestones and compounding. Universal Paperclips: shifts in scale and mechanics. Antimatter Dimensions: layered reset rewards. Melvor Idle: interconnected timers and offline progression. NGU Idle: flexible allocation. Bitburner / Terminal.exe: terminal hacker economy. OpenFront: commitment + automatic battles + readable losses. Android: Netrunner: defenders control secrecy and defenses, attackers choose where to probe.

## Success criteria
After 10 minutes the player understands the basic economic loop, has won/lost at least one meaningful breach, and can identify a next unlock. After 60 minutes, automation has reduced repetition and a new strategic choice has emerged. Both factions should feel substantively different in a blind playtest.

## Explicitly unresolved
Final product name/legal clearance, sound and font licenses, exact Steam host framework, localization languages, final price, final prestige pacing, full Corp content. These are decisions for testing, not blocking core prototype.
